# Sentinel-Agent: a private course report draft

## Problem

Flood evacuation is a sequence of decisions made with incomplete and changing information. A route that appears open at dispatch time may become inaccessible while a group is travelling. A shelter may have space in a static map but a changing reservation or local hazard can make that apparent capacity unreliable. An operator needs a picture of roads, demand, shelter state and decision evidence at the same moment. Sentinel-Agent is a research prototype that brings those pieces into one simulated incident. Its roads are drawn from the real Fort Kochi network; its flood and operational conditions are simulated. The work is not operationally validated. It must not be used to direct a real evacuation.

The project began with a practical question: can an incident operator inspect a defensible suggestion and the evidence behind it before choosing an action? The prototype responds with a visible recommendation, alternatives, a decision record and an audit trail. It treats a hold as an explicit action, so uncertainty remains visible. The operator can inspect the state of waiting groups, groups already on the road, shelter availability and recent events. The interface carries the honesty statement on operational pages, because road realism must not be mistaken for validation of the hazard model or the decision policy.

The project also addresses a research question about what evidence is sufficient to describe the behavior of a decision system. Planner results, retrieval results, claim-support verification, certificate replay and a preregistered comparison were conducted as separate experiments. Each has a different unit of analysis and a different limit on interpretation. A planner score from a synthetic held-out suite does not validate a physical deployment. A retrieval hit measures whether a judged passage was found, not whether a disaster-management rule was correctly converted into an action. A certificate replay establishes consistency with stored inputs, not the truth of those inputs. Keeping these statements separate is central to this report.

Fort Kochi is a useful geographic setting because it forces the interface to show a connected urban road network, zones and shelters rather than abstract boxes. The use of real topology is also a source of risk in communication: a screenshot can look operational even when the floods and groups are invented. The draft therefore identifies the simulated conditions in the opening, the method, the results and the user guide. The recorded guided run is a demonstration of the engine's behavior under one chosen seed. It is not a performance sample. The live session is an interactive simulation, with a human choice at each step and continuing checks on groups in transit. Neither mode gives permission to claim real-world effectiveness.

This work is a course submission draft under restricted disclosure. The student must review any text, figure and recording before release. The report describes the research problem, construction of the simulator and console, experiments performed, what passed, what failed, and how the system should be interpreted. The evidence ledger beside this report records exactly where each quoted result comes from. The limitation established by the preregistered evaluation is explicit: there is **no outcome-effect claim**. The project may discuss observed decision behavior and replay properties, while keeping the failed system-level bar in view. [6]

## Literature

The starting point is disaster-management guidance and road-network data. The local National Disaster Management Authority source discusses temporary shelter and related response considerations. Its passages are kept with page and chunk identifiers so a proposed document rule can be checked against the source rather than accepted because its wording sounds plausible. The source does not, by itself, authorize a numeric evacuation threshold in the simulator. In the current rule-tiering record, both approved rules remain at the lower tier. The report does not turn a qualitative passage into a stronger claim than the passage supports. [1][8]

OpenStreetMap supplies the road topology used to make the Fort Kochi interface geographically concrete. The graph is a model of connected roads and locations; it is not a current traffic or flood feed. A road graph can help ask whether a route is connected and can place a candidate in a meaningful visual setting. It cannot establish that a road will stay passable during an actual disaster. The repository records the extract and graph-processing history, while the console presents the resulting geometry with simulated conditions clearly marked. [2]

The project's earlier policy study compares learned and non-learned planners on frozen synthetic scenarios. That work is relevant because it shows why a plausible learned policy is not automatically the best operational suggestion. The held-out study reports a practical normalized safety utility of **1.531** for the stronger lookahead planner, **1.000** for the principal risk-greedy comparator, and **0.209** for the mean of the evaluated reinforcement-learning seeds. These values belong to that frozen test and its metric definition; they should not be compared with a real evacuation outcome. The learned policy did not meet the preregistered benefit gate. The literature lesson for this project is methodological: make the comparison rule, baseline and limits visible before treating an algorithmic output as a benefit. [3][5]

The retrieval study asks a different question. A user may need a relevant passage from the prepared document corpus, but a retrieval ranking is not a legal or operational validation of the statement drawn from that passage. On the frozen, AI-assisted labeled benchmark, direct hit at the stated cutoff was **86.7 %** for BM25, **82.2 %** for the hybrid method and **62.2 %** for dense retrieval. The hybrid method met its preregistered comparison against dense retrieval; BM25 was a strong descriptive comparator. The benchmark labels were AI assisted, with a documented coverage extension. They are not independently human-annotated gold data. These distinctions guide the console: a source passage and its provenance are evidence to inspect, not an automatic approval of a rule. [4]

Claim-support verification was added because a relevant passage can still fail to support a rule's exact wording. The failed predecessor evaluation, **14C failed**, with a macro score of **58.5 %** and a span-integrity failure rate of **10.4 %**. The later **14D evaluation of record** achieved its frozen T2-use verdict and was explicitly approved for that role. Its T2 precision was **100.0 %** on the construction-labeled test set. The older **14B superseded** evaluation was never scored. These are evaluations of a verifier against project challenges, not evidence that every future document claim is safe. The two currently approved NDMA rules each received a partially supported answer in the tiering run, so both remain T1. No rule is T2 in the current recorded result. [7][8]

The prototype also draws on ordinary engineering ideas: versioned contracts, deterministic fixtures, read-only result manifests, separation of implementation and acceptance checks, and accessible user interfaces. Their value is in allowing an auditor to ask what was actually run and what is still unavailable. A missing result is shown as a dash with a reason. A run result is displayed from its artifact rather than typed into the page. The course draft follows the same pattern by placing every quoted measurement in `NUMBERS.md` before repeating it here.

## Method

The system models an incident as zones with people awaiting movement, shelters with capacity and occupancy, roads with connectivity, and a time-stepped hazard process. The simulator makes the state observable at each decision step and records an audit trail. It exposes a distinction between a group waiting at a zone and a group already in transit. The network geometry is based on Fort Kochi roads, while all hazard states, population and incident events in this experiment are simulated. The model cannot substitute for field reports, official road closure updates or current shelter registers. [2]

In the initial policy work, a frozen scenario generator produced held-out episodes for several planners and the reinforcement-learning family. The project recorded both primary results and diagnostics, including latency and stress checks. The method kept training and held-out evaluation separate. The reinforcement-learning system was selected by a frozen validation rule before the held-out comparison. The resulting planner study is a useful negative result: its measured utility did not meet the stated advantage rule. The report includes it because selecting only favorable rows would make the technical story misleading. [3][5]

The document path begins with a prepared corpus and retrieval methods that rank candidate passages. The local rule file records a proposed predicate, parameters, strict fallback and an evidence reference for each rule. The rule-tiering result is read from its recorded file. The interface does not infer a higher tier from an approval record, a score or a general statement that the verifier is useful. The current result has NDMA-R01 and NDMA-R02 at T1 because the exact proposed numeric predicates were only partially supported by the cited passages. The tier can change only through a new recorded evaluation of the rule and its source. [1][4][8]

The restricted description of the dispatch gate is deliberately limited to the following approved paragraph:

> Before any dispatch is executed, Sentinel checks it against many sampled hazard futures and allows it only when a statistical lower bound on its success clears a fixed threshold; otherwise it holds and escalates to a human (fail-closed). Every decision gets a hash-chained certificate that anyone can replay bit-for-bit.

The prototype's FastAPI service connects the simulated session, recorded demonstration, certificates and evidence to a browser console. The guided run is generated from a fixed world, seed and demonstration parameter set and stored as a trace. Rebuilding the trace must give identical bytes, and each certificate in it is checked against its stored replay inputs. The browser can play the trace step by step, move backward, reset and inspect a certificate. Its tamper control changes a copy of one field for a replay demonstration; it does not alter the stored certificate. A separate live session permits the operator to consider a fresh certified context and make a recorded choice. Groups already on the road are re-checked as the simulation advances. These modes are clearly labeled so a viewer does not confuse a recorded choice with a new live one.

The presentation layer reads an allowlisted evidence manifest. It formats an available field from a frozen artifact and shows the artifact path; if a file or field is missing, it leaves the value blank with a reason. The document-tier table follows the same read-through rule. It shows the recorded tier only when the result still matches the approval and source-rule files. A stale result cannot be displayed as a current T2 rule. This design keeps explanatory wording close to what was observed, even when the underlying backend has richer detail than can be released under the current disclosure level.

Reproducibility in this prototype has a narrow meaning. The recorded demonstration is deterministic under its fixed configuration. A certificate can be replayed from its stored inputs to check that the recorded decision computation matches. The frozen study result files can be read and their printed figures checked against their documented pointers. None of these checks guarantees that an unobserved real hazard, a field report or a later data source is correct. The course pack preserves the chain from result file to quoted number so the student can audit wording before submitting any version.

## Experiments

The earlier policy and retrieval experiments are retained as background rather than rerun for this report. Their frozen methods and limitations are documented separately. The policy study used held-out synthetic scenarios and compared planners and a learned policy under preregistered rules. The retrieval study used a frozen AI-assisted benchmark and reported direct-hit and ranking metrics. Their scores are quoted here only at the precision represented in the source files and the numbers ledger. [3][4][5]

The claim-support sequence includes a failed predecessor and a later evaluation of record. **14C failed** its required macro-score and span-integrity conditions. **14D** then ran a frozen, separately approved test-set protocol and became the evaluation of record for T2 use. The test labels were constructed for the project, not independent human gold. The user approved use of that verdict in the rule loader, but the loader still had to check each approved rule. The resulting NDMA-R01 and NDMA-R02 rows are T1 because the verifier answered partially supported for their precise thresholds. This separation between a useful verifier and a rule that actually passes it is an important experimental result. **14B superseded** has no score in the evidence table. [7][8]

Certificate replay was checked against a recorded batch of **1274** certified decisions, with each replay compared to its original record. This batch is a test of internal reproducibility. It does not show that the simulated futures or road inputs are calibrated to the real world. The in-transit test produced **854** recorded migrations and **0** returns to a previously left shelter under the tested policy. Those figures describe a bounded simulation exercise. They are not estimates of real traffic diversion or a claim about the experience of evacuees. [9]

The preregistered Phase 27 comparison is the decisive test of the larger effect statement. It set multiple bars before the final runs and reported pass and fail states without revising their thresholds afterward. The safety bar, Bar 4, passed. The outcome bar, Bar 1, failed because the evaluated gate delivered only **21.4 %** of the people delivered by its named comparator under the guard definition, below the **95 %** requirement. The gate dispatched far fewer groups in that comparison. A decision-level comparison passed, but its internal details remain withheld under restricted disclosure. The variance bar, Bar 3, failed. This report makes **no outcome-effect claim** from Phase 27. [6]

The course demonstration is a separate exercise. Its seed was chosen during planning because the first recorded steps show holds, dispatches and a re-route. That makes it useful for teaching the interface and audit sequence. It does not make it a representative sample of incident outcomes. The demonstration parameter set is the faster set used for a local demo, rather than the evaluated configuration. The video shot list therefore labels it as a recorded run and follows it with a live-session segment, where the operator can see a newly computed suggestion and its alternatives. The two shots illustrate functionality; they do not add a new outcome experiment.

The web acceptance checks cover navigation, mock-mode pages, accessibility checks, recorded run playback, certificate detail and replay, and the visible rule tier. The Python checks cover the trace schema, certificate chain, replay from stored inputs, API response contracts, missing and stale tier results, and the read-only evidence projection. These are software checks. They can detect a mismatch between implementation and contract; they cannot replace external validation or a field trial. Keeping those boundaries explicit prevents a passing test suite from being reported as an operational safety certification.

## Results

The strongest planner row in the frozen V3 comparison remained the lookahead policy's **1.531** practical normalized safety utility. The risk-greedy baseline was **1.000**, while the evaluated reinforcement-learning family mean was **0.209**. The learned system did not meet its preregistered benefit gate. These are values in a synthetic held-out evaluation, using that study's normalization, and not a direct measure of this console's dispatch gate. [3][5]

On the frozen retrieval benchmark, BM25 had a direct hit of **86.7 %**, hybrid **82.2 %**, and dense **62.2 %** at the reported cutoff. The formal hybrid-vs-dense verdict was supported under its preregistered criterion. That result should not be enlarged into a claim that the retrieved passage proves every downstream rule. The labeling and coverage limitations described in the frozen report still apply. [4]

The claim-support sequence illustrates both failure and correction. **14C failed**, with **58.5 %** macro score and **10.4 %** span-integrity failure. **14D is the evaluation of record** and met its frozen T2-use verdict, including **100.0 %** T2 precision on the project's construction-labeled challenge. **14B superseded** remains unscored. Approval of the 14D verifier enabled rule-specific checks; it did not promote every rule. The recorded tiering result shows NDMA-R01 and NDMA-R02 at T1, both with a partially supported answer. There is no recorded T2 rule. [7][8]

The replay batch includes **1274** certified decisions. Each was available for comparison against its stored input and certificate. The in-transit test recorded **854** migrations and **0** returns to a previously left shelter. Together these results support narrower engineering statements about repeatability and the behavior of the tested simulator configuration. They do not support an estimate of real-world routing performance. [9]

In Phase 27, the preregistered safety bar passed, the outcome bar failed, the decision-level comparison passed and the variance bar failed. The Bar 1 throughput guard was **21.4 %**, below **95 %**. That failed guard blocks an outcome-effect statement even though other internal decision comparisons passed. The system was too conservative in that evaluated configuration to meet the registered throughput condition. The failure is reported as a result, not hidden behind the pass of another bar. The present report therefore makes **no outcome-effect claim**. [6]

The recorded browser run demonstrates that a viewer can inspect a hold, later dispatches, a re-route, certificates and replay. Its final step is reached by guided playback, and its certificate detail can show a matching replay and a deliberate mismatch when the tamper control changes a copy. The live session shows a different interaction: the operator receives the current recommendation and alternatives and records a choice. These are observable features of the prototype. The demonstration is selected for clarity and cannot stand in for a representative evaluation sample.

## Limitations

The most important limitation is external validity. The roads are real, but the incident, hazard evolution, demand, shelter status and operator choices are simulated. A map can be geographically faithful while the decision inputs remain artificial. The prototype has no validation against an actual Fort Kochi flood response, no current official closure feed, and no authority to issue real dispatch orders. A console user should be trained to read the honesty banner and verify field information independently. Real deployment would require governance, updated data, operational testing and an accountable human decision chain.

The preregistered result limits system-level interpretation. Bar 1 failed because the evaluated gate dispatched far fewer groups and reached an R of **21.4 %**, below **95 %**. Reporting a smaller observed failure fraction without that throughput guard would be misleading. Bar 3 also failed, so the project does not claim a variance benefit from the relevant sampling comparison. The passed decision-level and safety bars remain useful technical observations, but they do not repair the failed outcome bar. This is why the report states **no outcome-effect claim** repeatedly rather than allowing a favorable screenshot or local test to imply otherwise. [6]

The V3 planner results belong to a different model and metric setting. The learned policy's five-seed aggregate was weaker than the named comparators on the registered practical utility criterion. The source study discusses representation and information differences between policies, as well as stress and latency checks. Those caveats prevent a simple story that one planning family is always superior. This report summarizes the frozen result rather than running another selection process or tuning the displayed policy after seeing the test set. [3][5]

The retrieval benchmark uses AI-assisted relevance labels. Its formal comparison was planned, but the labels are not independent human gold, and judged-pool recall is not exhaustive corpus recall. A direct hit is a retrieval metric, not a guarantee that the answer sentence supports a precise operational predicate. The claim-support evaluations themselves also rely on construction-labeled challenges. The failed **14C** result is part of the record, and **14D** is limited to its frozen configuration. **14B superseded** has no result to quote. Those constraints are especially important when considering a document-derived rule. [4][7]

Rule tiering is specific to the approved source and current recorded run. NDMA-R01 and NDMA-R02 are T1 after partially supported answers. The current local corpus did not supply a fully supported numeric evacuation threshold for those predicates. A higher tier would require a suitable source and a new recorded check. The interface therefore reads the tier result directly and suppresses tier values if the result is stale. It does not turn a successful verifier score into a display claim about individual rules. [1][8]

Software acceptance has its own limits. Schema validity and bit-exact replay show that data fields and deterministic computations agree with their contracts. They do not prove that an action is wise under an unknown real future. Browser accessibility tests catch important interaction defects but cannot establish that every operator will interpret the data correctly under stress. The hardware bridge can be simulated without producing a physical bench latency result. Missing physical results stay marked as unavailable instead of being filled with estimates. An incident command team would need independent review of controls, logging, failover and responsibilities before considering any operational use.

This draft has a disclosure limitation as well as a scientific one. The filing decision is on hold and the release level remains restricted. Mechanism details are intentionally absent from the outward-facing report, poster and narration plan. The student and project owner must approve the final text and decide what may be released. Until then, no screenshot, source extract or recording from this folder should leave the private project. The withheld material is listed in `DISCLOSURE.md` so that the boundary is visible to everyone preparing the submission.

## Ethics and safety

Evacuation support affects people with different mobility, medical needs, access to transport and trust in institutions. A simulator may encode an average travel time or a capacity number while hiding how unevenly those conditions are experienced. The interface should therefore encourage an operator to inspect alternatives and reasons rather than treat a computed suggestion as an order. A hold must be presented as a real decision with an escalation path, not as a silent failure. The user guide explains that the research console is a local simulated tool and cannot authorize dispatch in the field.

Data provenance matters ethically because a realistic map can lend false credibility to synthetic events. The project separates road geometry from simulated hazard conditions in the banner and page copy. The evidence page shows a source path and reason when a value is unavailable. The course draft uses a numbers ledger for the same purpose. A reader can trace each figure to a frozen file and see whether it was a planner benchmark, retrieval benchmark, claim-support challenge or gate experiment. That prevents a result from one task being casually transferred to another.

The rule-verification history is a case study in retaining negative evidence. **14C failed** and remains visible with its reasons. **14D evaluation of record** was run under a frozen replacement protocol, but its success does not erase the earlier failure or make every rule T2. **14B superseded** is labeled unscored. NDMA-R01 and NDMA-R02 remain T1 because the exact claimed thresholds were only partially supported. Showing that distinction is safer than reporting only a useful-verifier label. [7][8]

The preregistered Phase 27 outcome bar also requires honest restraint. Its failed throughput guard, **21.4 %** against **95 %**, means this report makes **no outcome-effect claim**. A safety-oriented decision system can still impose large costs in the number of people moved. Both kinds of result must travel together. The course poster and video use the same wording, so a short presentation cannot silently omit the limitation. The results are synthetic and do not estimate casualties, lives saved or a real response team's performance. [6]

The interactive live session adds a human decision record, including the chosen action and reason. That record supports accountability only if the user understands what was shown and if the simulated conditions are not mistaken for official information. The research interface provides comparison text and a refusal for an uncertified override; it is not a replacement for incident-command procedures. Local password protection and private repository access are additional boundaries while disclosure is restricted. They do not turn the console into a certified operational service.

The contribution log is intentionally a blank template. The student must state what they personally designed, ran, checked and wrote, including any AI assistance. An agent cannot claim those contributions for the student. Likewise, the user must review the final narrative, poster and video before any course submission. This preserves both academic integrity and the confidentiality decision.

## Future work

Future research should begin with the failure of Bar 1, not with an assumption that a more elaborate console will fix it. A new study could define a revised operating point or decision policy and preregister its own thresholds before running any final evaluation. It would need to measure both safety and movement throughput, because the prior result shows that one can look favorable while the other fails. Such work would be a new experiment, not a reinterpretation or silent rerun of the frozen Phase 27 result. [6]

External validation would require data and institutions outside this prototype. Road closures, shelter capacity and hazard updates would need current authoritative sources, and the uncertainty model would need calibration against real observations. Human factors testing could examine whether operators understand the difference between a hold, a certified alternative and a demonstration replay. It should include accessibility, time pressure and cases where the best available information is incomplete or contradictory. Any field-facing version would require explicit operational ownership and independent safety review.

The document path could be strengthened with a source that actually states an evacuation threshold relevant to a proposed predicate. The current NDMA passages support qualitative guidance but did not fully support the recorded numeric rules. A new source should be added with provenance and independently reviewed before any rule-tiering run. Improving the verifier alone would not solve a source gap. A better corpus and clearer human review of rule wording may be more valuable than treating a confidence score as authority. [1][8]

The retrieval and claim-support benchmarks also invite stronger labeling. The present retrieval study is AI assisted; independent domain-expert judgments would test how stable its rankings and relevance grades are. The claim-support challenge is construction labeled; a later human-labeled set could test whether the useful-verifier verdict transfers to less curated claims. Any such work should be frozen before model selection and should retain failed attempts. The report should continue to distinguish a supported benchmark comparison from evidence of real disaster-response utility. [4][7]

For the interface, the next steps are careful usability work and clearer comparison of alternatives, not additional display precision without evidence. The recorded run can teach the audit sequence, while a live session can expose what an operator must decide under uncertainty. A student might test whether readers can find the source of a number, recognize a stale tier result and identify the limits of a certificate replay. These are measurable questions about the presentation layer. They should not be used to imply that the underlying decision policy has solved the outcome problem.

This restricted draft is therefore a checkpoint. It documents the prototype, its frozen experiments, the corrected claim-support evaluation, current T1 rules, the failed outcome bar and the approval boundary. The project owner can decide whether to file, release more detail, run a new study or keep the work private. Until that decision is recorded, the course pack remains internal, and the scientific conclusion stays narrow: the system demonstrates auditable simulated decisions and recorded software behavior, with **no outcome-effect claim**.
